## 1. Voice & Tone

**Kerinti speaks like a calm, experienced partner, not a hype-driven startup.**

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Clear, warm, confident | Loud, buzzword-heavy ("revolutionary AI-powered synergy") |
| Concrete about restaurant life (tables, tickets, shifts, stock) | Abstract ("unlock your potential") |
| Short sentences, strong verbs | Long feature lists in prose |
| Respectful of the people who work in restaurants | Talking down to operators |

**Copy rules for the scroll experience**
- One headline + at most one supporting line on screen during pinned scenes.
- Headlines ≤ 10 words (EN), ≤ 12 words (TR).
- Each Ch4 beat line describes a **result**, not a feature name.
- Feature names appear in eyebrows or margin notes, never as the headline.

## 10. Turkish Adaptation Notes (general)

1. **Length.** Turkish runs ~15–25% longer. Every text container is designed for the TR version first, and headlines have TR-specific line breaks.
2. **Formality.** Use **"siz"** consistently (Demo Talep Edin, görün, okutun). Avoid mixing with "sen" forms.
3. **Hospitality vocabulary.** Prefer "misafir" over "müşteri" in guest-facing contexts; "işletme" for the business owner audience.
4. **Industry terms.** Use the glossary in §5. Operators say "adisyon," "cari," and "kasa," so don't over-translate into formal alternatives.
5. **Capitalization.** Turkish dotted/dotless i: `İ/i` and `I/ı`. Uppercase transforms (eyebrows) must use `lang="tr"` so CSS `text-transform: uppercase` renders "İ" correctly. Verify fonts render ğ, ş, ı, İ properly at all weights.
6. **Rhythm devices.** Triads work well in both languages ("Okut. İncele. Sipariş ver." / "Bir sipariş. Bir gün. Tek sistem.").
7. **Legal.** The Turkish site needs KVKK-compliant form consent text and a privacy notice link (content to be provided by Kerinti's legal/compliance side).
8. **Native review.** All TR copy here is a draft and should be reviewed by a native-speaking copywriter familiar with the restaurant sector before launch.
